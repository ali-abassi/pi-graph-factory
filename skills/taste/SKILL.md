# Taste and product-UI floor

Read taste context in this order: explicit approved plan, approved references,
project product/design system, project `TASTE.md`, then general platform
conventions. A scoped taste file never governs an unrelated product merely
because it exists.

Before editing, state one concise taste read: the artifact/register, audience and
usage moment, desired feeling, and failure mode to avoid. Lock `use`, `avoid`,
and `prove` decisions for composition, type, spacing, color/material, imagery,
motion, and component treatment. Name one product-specific signature move and
the five most likely generic model defaults for this surface.

Reject unearned defaults: generic centered heroes, repeated equal-card grids,
indiscriminate pills, nested dashboard cards, ornamental status dots, arbitrary
gradient/pastel/neon palettes, fake product evidence, emoji soup, and system icons
standing in for central product art. A familiar convention may stay when it
serves the audience or platform; cite why.

Function and accessibility are the floor, not taste dimensions. At real default
and minimum native viewports verify:

- no clipping, overlap, unreachable content, or accidental wrapping;
- the primary job and action are visible or immediately discoverable;
- representative long text, dynamic type, touch/focus targets, and safe areas;
- normal, maximum-content, empty, loading, error, degraded, and disabled states;
- truthful feedback, inspectable critical content, and working primary actions;
- reduced motion and the relevant native platform/window behavior.

For new or contested direction, compare matched alternatives at identical
viewport, content, and state. Prefer direct A/B evidence and reverse presentation
order when practical. Do not call something polished, distinctive, premium, or
intuitive without visible evidence. A floor failure, missing state, blank image,
or unobserved primary interaction is `Rework`, regardless of aesthetic score.

Preserve what already works. Spend revisions on the few highest-leverage visible
problems, then re-render the same states. Taste is contextual judgment; it never
overrides an approved outcome, target-user evidence, accessibility, or a failed
deterministic check.
