# Decision discipline

Use this when a plan must choose among meaningful product, architecture,
platform, proof, or resource options. Do not turn a cheap reversible choice into
ceremony.

1. State the objective, horizon, hard constraints, unacceptable outcomes,
   reversibility, cost of delay, and action the decision enables.
2. Separate decision-driving inputs into `FACT`, `FORECAST`, `VALUE`, and
   `UNKNOWN`. Never let model confidence substitute for evidence or user values.
3. Generate genuinely distinct options before selecting one. Include a staged
   test or deferral only when it is feasible and informative.
4. Screen veto conditions before scoring tradeoffs. Account for implementation,
   tool, integration, review, error, latency, switching, and opportunity costs.
5. Use the outside view and research only facts that could change the choice.
   Seek credible disconfirmation, not several sources repeating one claim.
6. Prefer the smallest reversible choice that still achieves the requested
   outcome. Record the largest vulnerability and the evidence or threshold that
   would switch the decision.

The plan's `research` and `assumptions` fields are the durable decision record.
Do not expose private chain-of-thought or invent numerical weights. Under judge
authority, resolve safely reversible uncertainty from the request, project
memory, repository, and inspected evidence; ask a person only when the factory
contract explicitly selects human governance.
